Power BI

ShopNest Sales Data Analysis Dashboard

Problem
Retail data covered 99,441 orders from 2016–2018 across sales, payments, delivery, ratings and regional trends.

Analysis
Built an interactive Power BI dashboard using Power Query, DAX and a star-schema-style model to investigate eight business questions.

Insight
Revenue reached $15.84M with an average rating of 4.09/5. São Paulo led with $5.20M; credit cards accounted for 73.9% of payments. Bed/Bath/Table had 811 delayed orders.

Impact
Consolidated key performance measures into one dashboard and surfaced categories and regions for further sales and delivery investigation.

SQL · PostgreSQL

Customer Retention & Cohort Analysis

Problem
Historical retail transactions needed to explain repeat purchasing, retention timing and revenue contribution from returning customers.

Analysis
Analyzed 805,531 purchase lines from 5,878 customers using PostgreSQL CTEs, window functions, cohort logic and purchase-timing calculations.

Insight
71.27% of customers purchased on multiple days. Median time to a second purchase was 63 days; repeat customers generated £16.74M, or 96.04% of gross revenue. Month-one retention ranged from 9.21% to 32.13%.

Impact
Identified retention patterns and a potential 30–90-day re-engagement window worth testing; the analysis does not prove a campaign would improve retention.

Online Retail Sales & Customer Analysis

Problem
A dataset of 541,909 transactions needed structured reporting across sales, customers, products, geography, time trends and cancellations.

Analysis
Created an 11-sheet Excel workbook with an executive dashboard, sales and customer analysis, product and country views, returns analysis and data-quality documentation.

Insight
Gross sales were $10.67M across 5.59M units; average order value was $534.40. November 2011 peaked at about $1.51M, while the UK contributed 84.6% of gross sales.

Impact
Organized the analysis into a business-friendly workbook for reviewing sales trends, customer behavior, geographic concentration and data-quality limitations.

Python

Project Cost & Employee Data Analysis

Problem
Employee, seniority and project data were split across three datasets: 5 employees, 5 seniority records and 14 projects, with two missing project costs.

Analysis
Used Pandas and NumPy to clean and merge data, estimate missing costs with a running average, calculate 5% bonuses for completed projects, apply designation rules and aggregate costs by employee.

Insight
The largest calculated project bonus was 225,000. Total project costs were 26.4 million; Nina accounted for 9.5 million (about 36%). Totals include estimated missing costs.

Impact
Produced a consolidated dataset with employee-level cost summaries, calculated bonuses and rule-based designation updates for structured project-cost analysis.
